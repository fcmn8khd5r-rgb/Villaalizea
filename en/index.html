The place

A low, open house,
set on the point.

Villa Alizéa sits at the far end of a plot in Terres Basses, the residential quarter that closes off the western tip of Saint-Martin between Baie Longue and Baie Rouge. The living room does not shut: it carries on into a covered terrace, a pool, then the garden of sand and coconut palms.

The trade wind — the alizé — crosses the house from morning to evening. It gave the place its name, and it is why the house was drawn without a corridor or a needless wall.

Around

Saint-Martin fits
into a single morning.

Thirty-seven square kilometres shared between France and the Netherlands, with no marked border: you cross from one to the other without noticing. From Terres Basses, Marigot is twelve minutes away, Grand-Case twenty, and Juliana airport fifteen.
